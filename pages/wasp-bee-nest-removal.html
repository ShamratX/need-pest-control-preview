Wasp and bee nest removal in Kitchener

Wasp nest under the eaves or bees by the door? We remove nests from eaves, siding, door frames and decks so you can use your yard again.

Wasp nests start small in spring and grow fast through summer, so a nest under the eaves or behind the siding can be large by August. Bees swarm near walls, decks and doorways.

We remove the nest so you can use your door, deck and yard again.

Signs you have wasps and bees

  • Wasps flying in and out of one spot on the siding or eaves
  • A papery nest under the eaves, deck or a door frame
  • Wasps or bees inside the house near a wall or window
  • A bee swarm resting on a wall, fence or tree

  1. LocateWe find the nest and how it’s attached or where it’s hidden.
  2. RemoveThe nest is removed safely.
  3. CheckWe look for other nests around the house.

The wasps you are most likely to meet in Kitchener

Three kinds cause most calls in southern Ontario: paper wasps, which build the open, umbrella-shaped nests under eaves and deck rails; bald-faced hornets, which build the large grey football-shaped nests; and yellowjackets, which often nest in the ground, in wall gaps or behind siding. Honey bees and bumble bees usually swarm or nest differently, so tell us what you are seeing when you call.

Why a nest grows so fast

A queen starts a nest alone in spring with a handful of cells. Her first workers hatch and help her build, and the colony grows every week through summer. By late summer a nest that began as a golf-ball-sized start can hold hundreds of wasps, and they are more defensive around it. Removing a nest in early summer is almost always easier than in September.

What not to do

Most stings happen when someone tries to deal with a nest alone.

  • Don’t plug the entrance to a nest in a wall or soffit. Wasps can chew through drywall and end up inside the house.
  • Don’t knock a nest down, hose it or use a flame. Disturbed wasps attack in numbers.
  • Don’t approach a nest at dusk with a bright light unless you know what you’re doing. The wasps are all home then and are drawn to the light.
  • If someone has trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat or feels faint after a sting, call 911.

Keeping nests from coming back

Wasps often rebuild in the same sheltered spots. After the nest is removed, you can reduce the odds of a new one by closing gaps in siding and soffits, keeping garbage and green-bin lids shut and cleaning up sweet spills on decks in summer. If wasps have been getting in through a gap in the siding or soffit, our damage repair work closes it.

Questions about wasps and bees

Should I spray or block the nest myself?

It’s safer not to. Blocking an opening in the siding can push wasps inside the house, and disturbing a nest can lead to stings.

When are wasp nests biggest?

By late summer. Removing a nest earlier in the season is usually easier.

Do you remove bee hives and wasp nests?

We remove wasp and bee nests from eaves, siding, door frames and decks. Tell us what you are seeing when you call so we can plan the visit.

What if the nest is inside the wall?

Don’t plug the entry hole. Call us: a nest inside a wall needs a plan, since sealing the opening can push wasps into the house.

Is it safe to stay in the house?

Usually, yes, as long as the nest is outside and the entrance isn’t inside. If wasps are coming into the house, call us right away.
