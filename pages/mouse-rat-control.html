Mouse and rat control in Kitchener

Droppings, gnaw marks or scratching in the walls? We remove mice and rats and seal the gaps they use to get in.

Mice fit through very small gaps in the foundation and around pipes and cables. Rats follow food and shelter into garages and basements. Both move indoors most often when the weather turns cold.

We remove them and close the places they use to get in.

Signs you have mice and rats

  • Droppings in cupboards, drawers or along walls
  • Gnaw marks on food packaging, wood or cables
  • Scratching inside walls or ceilings at night
  • Nests made of shredded paper or insulation
  • Grease marks along the bottom of walls

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe find the activity and the gaps around the foundation, pipes and doors.
  2. RemoveMice or rats are removed.
  3. SealEntry gaps are sealed so new ones can’t follow.

How mice and rats get in

A mouse can squeeze through a gap about the width of a pencil. Rats need a bigger opening, but a rat can still gnaw a small hole larger. The usual routes are cracks in the foundation, gaps around pipes and cables where they enter the house, worn door sweeps on garages, dryer and bathroom vents without screens, and the spaces where the roof meets the walls.

Why they come inside in fall

When nights turn cold, mice and rats look for warmth, food and nesting material. Homes that stay warm and have food in pantries, garages or pet bowls are the obvious choice. Activity inside tends to start with one or two sightings and grows quickly, because mice breed fast.

Why traps alone don’t end it

Catching the mice you can see is only half the problem. If the way in stays open, new mice arrive. Poison is also a poor answer indoors: an animal can die inside a wall and smell for weeks, and pets and wildlife can be harmed by poisoned mice. Our approach is to remove the animals and seal the openings, which is what stops the cycle.

Droppings and cleanup

Mouse and rat droppings can spread illness, including through dust. Don’t sweep or vacuum dry droppings. Wear gloves and a mask, wet the area with disinfectant first and wipe it up with paper towels, then wash your hands well. If the droppings are in the attic or insulation, leave it to us.

Help prevent the next visit

Small habits matter:

  • Store pantry food, pet food and bird seed in hard containers.
  • Keep firewood and clutter away from the foundation.
  • Fit door sweeps on exterior doors and garage doors.
  • Seal gaps around pipes and cables where they enter the house.

Questions about mice and rats

When do mice come indoors?

Most often in fall, as nights get cold and they look for warmth and food.

How do I know if it’s mice or rats?

Droppings are the clearest clue: rat droppings are noticeably larger. Tell us what you’ve found and we’ll know what to look for.

How quickly do mice multiply?

Mice breed quickly, which is why a few sightings can turn into an infestation within weeks. Acting early limits the damage.

Is poison a good idea?

We don’t recommend it indoors. Animals can die inside walls and cause odours, and poison can harm pets and wildlife. Removing the animals and sealing the openings is more reliable.

What attracts mice and rats?

Food, shelter and warmth: unsealed pantry food, pet food, bird seed and clutter near the foundation.
