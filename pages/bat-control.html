Bat control in Kitchener

Bats in the attic or walls? Timing matters in Ontario.

Bats roost in attics, behind soffits and in wall gaps, and a small gap is enough for them to get in. They are quiet, so most people find out from droppings or from bats flying out of the same spot at dusk.

Timing matters. The Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources timing table for bat exclusions says that from mid-May to mid-August flightless pups may be inside, and blocking the openings then can leave them separated from their mothers (read the guidance). Call us before you seal anything.

Signs you have bats

  • Droppings under the roofline or in the attic
  • Squeaking or scratching in the walls or attic around dusk and dawn
  • Bats flying out of the same spot on the house at dusk
  • Dark staining or a musty smell around an opening
  • A bat inside the living space: open the doors and windows so it can leave, and don’t handle it

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe look for the openings bats use and where they roost, outside and in the attic.
  2. Plan the timingWe check the time of year, since pups may be present from mid-May to mid-August, and work out what can be done now.
  3. ExcludeOnce the timing is right, openings are set up so bats can leave but not get back in.
  4. Seal and repairEntry points are sealed and soiled insulation is replaced.

Bats are protected, and timing is the whole job

Several of Ontario’s bats, including the little brown myotis, northern myotis and tri-colored bat, are at risk, and the federal government lists as endangered. That is why bat work isn’t like squirrel or raccoon work. The aim is to let the bats leave on their own and keep them out, not to harm them.

Ontario’s bat exclusion timing table advises that from mid-May to mid-August flightless pups are in the roost, and blocking the openings then separates them from their mothers. April to early May and late August to November are the windows for one-way doors, and the colder months suit sealing once the bats have left.

How to tell you have bats and not mice

Bat droppings are small, dry and crumbly, and they are often found in the same place under an opening, because bats return to the same entrance every night. Mouse droppings are smaller and rounder. You may also hear squeaking and scratching at dusk and dawn, or see dark staining around a gap on the outside of the house.

If a bat gets into the living space

Close interior doors, open a window or exterior door and let it leave. Don’t try to handle it. If it can’t get out, or if a bat was in a room with someone sleeping, a young child or a pet, contact your local public health unit, since bats can carry rabies and the animal may need to be tested.

What a bat job involves

We start with an inspection to find the openings and the roost. Then we check the date against the timing guidance and tell you what can be done now and what has to wait. Once the timing is right, we set up one-way exits so bats can leave but not return, and then seal the openings and replace soiled insulation. See our damage repair page for what is involved.

Questions about bats

Can I seal the hole myself?

Not in summer. From about mid-May to mid-August flightless pups may be inside, and sealing the opening can separate them from their mothers. Call us first.

What should I do about a bat in the house?

Open the doors and windows and give it time to leave on its own. The Ontario Ministry of Natural Resources does not recommend handling bats, so call us if it doesn’t leave.

When can bats be excluded?

Ontario’s timing table lists April to early May as a good time for one-way doors, and mid-May to mid-August as the period to avoid. Call us and we’ll tell you what applies to your home.

Are bats protected in Ontario?

Several Ontario bat species are at risk, which is why we follow the Ontario timing guidance and focus on letting bats leave and keeping them out.

How do I know if I have bats?

Look for small dry droppings under an opening, dark staining around a gap and bats leaving the same spot at dusk.

Is bat guano dangerous?

Dust from droppings can be a health concern in an attic. Don’t clean it up yourself. Call us and we’ll tell you how it is handled.
