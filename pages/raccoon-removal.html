Raccoon removal in Kitchener

Raccoons in the attic, chimney or roof? We remove them, replace soiled insulation and seal the entry point, and the work is guaranteed.

Raccoons are strong enough to pull up shingles, tear open roof vents and push through soffits to reach a warm attic. Once inside they flatten and soil the insulation, and the opening they made stays open for the next one.

We find where they are getting in, remove them, clean up the damage and seal the entry point, so the problem ends with the visit.

Signs you have raccoons

  • Heavy thumping or walking sounds in the ceiling at night
  • Torn shingles, lifted roof vents or a pushed-in soffit
  • Droppings or a strong smell in the attic
  • Flattened or stained insulation
  • Chirping in spring, when raccoons often have their young in attics and chimneys

How we handle it

  1. InspectWe check the roofline, chimney, soffits and the attic to find every way in.
  2. RemoveThe raccoons come out, including young in spring, and the den is cleared.
  3. RepairSoiled insulation is removed and replaced.
  4. SealThe entry points are closed off so another raccoon can’t reuse them.

Why raccoons end up in Kitchener attics

A raccoon needs three things: food, water and a safe, dry place to sleep or raise young. Houses near mature trees, fences, garbage and green-bin carts and backyard decks give raccoons food and an easy route up to the roof. From there, a chimney without a cap, a loose roof vent or a weak soffit is all they need.

Raccoons use their hands the way we do. They pull up shingle edges, peel back vent covers and tear at soft or rotted fascia. That is why a raccoon in the attic is rarely just a noise problem: there is almost always a damaged opening to repair as well.

From spring to early fall, assume there are babies

Toronto Wildlife Centre advises treating any raccoon living in an attic between January and September as a mother with young. Kits can’t leave on their own for their first weeks, so a mother whose way in is blocked will often tear into the roof to reach them.

This is the reason we inspect before we seal anything. If young are present, the mother and her kits are dealt with together, and the opening is only closed once the den is empty.

What not to do about raccoons in the attic

Most of the damage we repair started with a well-meant fix. Avoid these:

  • Don’t block the hole before you know the attic is empty. Animals left inside will chew a new way out.
  • Don’t use poison, mothballs or loud radios. They don’t reliably move a raccoon, and poison can leave an animal dying inside the ceiling.
  • Don’t go into the attic to clean up droppings. Raccoon droppings can carry roundworm eggs that make people ill if they are breathed in or swallowed.
  • Don’t plan on trapping the raccoon and driving it somewhere else. Ontario rules require live-captured wildlife to be released close to where it was caught (see Ontario’s rules on wildlife that damage property), so the lasting fix is closing the opening, not moving the animal.

What a lasting fix looks like

Removing the raccoon is the start. A lasting repair closes every opening with material an animal can’t chew or pull apart, replaces insulation that has been flattened and soiled, and checks the other weak points on the roofline so a second raccoon doesn’t simply pick another spot. That is why every raccoon job here includes animal damage repair.

Questions about raccoons

Can I clean up raccoon droppings myself?

It’s safer not to. Raccoon droppings can carry parasites, so leave the attic cleanup to us and keep pets and children out of the area.

Why do raccoons choose attics?

An attic is dry, warm and hard for predators to reach. In spring, mother raccoons look for exactly that kind of space to raise their young.

Is the work guaranteed?

Yes. If you hear animals again during the guarantee period, call and we’ll come back to check. Ask us for the guarantee details when you book.

What time of year do raccoons have babies in attics?

Mothers commonly look for dens in late winter and spring, and young can be in an attic into the summer. Toronto Wildlife Centre advises assuming a raccoon in an attic between January and September has young.

Will you relocate the raccoon?

Ontario rules require live-captured wildlife to be released close to where it was caught, so driving a raccoon far away isn’t an option. The lasting fix is removing the animals and sealing the opening so they can’t return.

How do I know it’s a raccoon and not a squirrel?

Raccoons are heavier and more active at night. Squirrels are lighter and usually active during the day. Our attic noises guide has a quick comparison.
