What’s that noise in the attic?

Most calls start with a sound in the ceiling. The time of day, the kind of noise and the time of year narrow it down quickly. Use this guide to work out what is probably up there, then call us to confirm.

Match the sound to the animal
What you hearMost likely
Heavy thumping, dragging or thudding, mostly at nightRaccoons
Scratching and running, starting around sunriseSquirrels
Squeaking and rustling at dusk and dawnBats
Light scratching in walls or ceilings at nightMice or rats
Buzzing near one spot on the wall or eaves in warm weatherWasps or bees

Why the time of day matters

Squirrels are active in daylight, raccoons are mostly active at night, and bats leave at dusk and return before dawn. A noise that starts when the sun rises usually points to squirrels. Noise that picks up after dark and sounds heavy is more likely a raccoon.

The time of year matters too

In late winter and spring, raccoons and squirrels look for a safe place to raise young. In summer, bat mothers are raising pups. In fall, mice, rats and squirrels move in as nights get cold. Winter noises usually mean animals that got in earlier in the season are still there. Our season guide shows what Kitchener homes typically face each time of year.

What to do first

Before you call, note what you can:

  • When you hear it (time of day) and how long it has been going on.
  • What it sounds like: thumping, scratching, squeaking or chewing.
  • Where it is: above a bedroom, in a wall or near the chimney.
  • Any droppings, stains, smells or damage you can see from outside.

What not to do

Don’t seal the hole you think they’re using until you know the attic is empty, especially from spring to early fall. Young animals may be inside, and a mother who can’t reach them will try to tear a new way in. Don’t use poison, and don’t clean up droppings yourself, since they can carry parasites and spread illness.
